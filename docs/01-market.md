## The price mechanism

`_commit_unit` (kaggriculture.py:630) quotes each sold unit at the **pre-sell** inventory, then
raises inventory by one — but only if the price cleared above the $1 floor:

```python
if op == "SELL":
    private["shed"][item] -= 1
    farm["money"] += price
    if price > 1:                      # sales at the floor do NOT add supply
        market["inventory"][item] += 1
```

That exception is load-bearing. Once a product bottoms out, dumping more into it doesn't dig
the hole deeper, so the pool recovers on town demand alone. Flooding a market is embarrassing
but not permanently self-harming.

`_process_market` (kaggriculture.py:521) settles both players in per-unit lockstep, quoting
both at the *same* pre-commit inventory before committing either. Simultaneous selling is
genuine congestion, not an artifact of who moved first.

## Three conclusions

**1. Fertilizer is the only truly fixed pool in the game.** No shop demands it, and
`TOWN_CENTER_PRODUCTS` explicitly excludes it. $25,045, regenerating at exactly zero, shared
with the opponent. It is also free: every surviving animal emits one per day whether or not it
was fed or cared for. ~20 animals over 25 days is the entire pool at zero marginal cost. This
is the single sharpest edge found so far, and it is a pure race.

**2. Melon is the only *crop* pool that doesn't regenerate meaningfully.** No shop demands it;
the town centre supplies 140 units/season against a depth of 158. So roughly 300 melon units
exist for the whole game across *both* players. Worth contesting early, worthless to contest
late.

**3. Everything else is shop-contingent, and the shop order is random.** Wool's pool is 59
units deep but the Yarn Store alone drains 12/day — over 300 units across a season, pushing
inventory below `I0` where wool sells *above* its $200 base. Whether a premium crop is worth
planting is a function of `town["unlocked_shops"]`, which is in the observation and which the
current agent ignores entirely.

The corollary for shallow products: with an active shop, sell *at the drain rate*, not in
bulk. Dumping 59 wool at once floors it; selling 20/day into 20/day of demand holds it near
base indefinitely.

## What this does not yet answer

Whether we can *produce* enough of any of this to hit the limits above. That is A2 (labour
accounting) — the binding constraint has not been established, and it may well be worker-turns
rather than market depth, in which case the shallow pools never bind and this analysis matters
less than it appears.
