# A4 — The contested pools as two-player games

Generated by `python -m analysis.pools`. Melon and fertilizer are the two products the town does
not meaningfully refill, so whatever the opponent takes is gone. A3 drained both to near their
caps assuming nobody else was selling; this is the correction.

## Method

`race()` replays the interpreter's own settlement: each round quotes *both* players at the same
pre-commit inventory, commits both, and a unit sold at the $1 floor doesn't add supply.

Payoffs are stated as **(my revenue − their revenue)**, net of what the same labour would have
earned in the uncontested egg engine ($14.72 per worker-turn). The ladder scores win/loss only,
so the difference is what the game is actually about — and stating it that way makes the
sub-game exactly zero-sum, which is why a maximin solve is meaningful here at all.

## Melon: commit 8 tiles, not 11

|  | opp 0 | opp 4 | opp 8 | opp 11 | opp 16 | opp 22 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **0** | 0 | −10,564 | −18,343 | −20,702 | −18,603 | −15,708 |
| **4** | 10,564 | 0 | −2,182 | −744 | 1,670 | 4,566 |
| **8** | 18,343 | 2,182 | **0** | 1,447 | 3,861 | 6,757 |
| **11** | 20,702 | 744 | −1,447 | 0 | 2,414 | 5,310 |
| **16** | 18,603 | −1,670 | −3,861 | −2,414 | 0 | 2,896 |
| **22** | 15,708 | −4,566 | −6,757 | −5,310 | −2,896 | 0 |

**Equilibrium: 8 tiles, pure.** Row 8 is a best response to every column. A3's 11 tiles loses
$1,447 against an equilibrium opponent — over-committing floods the pool you're trying to
harvest, and the extra labour would have earned more running geese.

Note how badly *conceding* does: committing zero melon against an 11-tile opponent costs
$20,702. This is not a pool you can ignore.

## The first-mover premium dominates everything

| | player 1 | player 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 11 tiles each, simultaneous harvest | $13,366 | $13,366 |
| 11 tiles each, we sell first | **$26,156** | $459 |

Selling into an untouched melon curve is worth **$12,790** more than splitting it — larger than
any other single decision found in Phase A. Melon needs 10 days to first yield, so this is
decided in the first day or two of the season and cannot be recovered later.

Practical consequence for Phase B: **melon goes in the ground immediately**, and the harvest
should be sold as soon as it lands rather than stockpiled for a better price. Waiting is how
you hand the curve to your opponent.

## Fertilizer: collect from ~8 animals, not 20

|  | opp 0 | opp 8 | opp 16 | opp 20 | opp 28 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **0** | 0 | −12,706 | −18,039 | −17,941 | −15,330 |
| **8** | 12,706 | **0** | 1,392 | 2,709 | 5,344 |
| **16** | 18,039 | −1,392 | 0 | 1,317 | 3,952 |
| **20** | 17,941 | −2,709 | −1,317 | 0 | 2,635 |
| **28** | 15,330 | −5,344 | −3,952 | −2,635 | 0 |

**Equilibrium: 8 animals' worth of collection.** A3 put the animal count at 20 precisely
because 20 × 24 days ≈ the 493-unit pool — but that was the monopolist's answer.

Read this carefully: it does **not** say keep only 8 animals. Animals also produce eggs, milk
and wool, and eggs are unbounded and uncontested. It says stop spending a
`COLLECT_FERTILIZER` action on every animal every day. Past roughly 8 animals' worth, that
action earns less than the $14.72/turn the same worker makes on eggs, *and* it accelerates the
flood. Keep the fleet, throttle the collection.

## Don't play the equilibrium — best-respond

Verified in `analysis/verify.py`: the opponent's crop choice and `planted_day` are both public.
So there is no reason to play a blind equilibrium strategy. The matrix above is a best-response
table, and the agent should index into it by counting the opponent's melon tiles:

| opponent melon tiles | our best response | gain |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 11 | +20,702 |
| 4 | 8 | +2,182 |
| 8 | 8 | 0 |
| 11 | 8 | +1,447 |
| 16 | 8 | +3,861 |
| 22 | 8 | +6,757 |

8 tiles is right against everything except an opponent who concedes entirely, where 11 is
better. The equilibrium is worth knowing as a floor; the observable state is worth more.

## Caveats

1. **Selling is modelled as continuous and simultaneous at equal rates.** Real harvests are
   lumpy, which is exactly what makes the head-start result so large — and means the true
   variance around these numbers is wide.
2. **Fertilizer collection is priced only against the egg alternative.** An agent that is
   labour-idle should collect anyway; the equilibrium assumes labour is scarce, which A3 says
   it is at the optimum.
3. **Two-player only.** The ladder pairs you against many opponents, but each episode is
   two-player, so this is the right frame.
