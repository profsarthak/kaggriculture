# A2 — Labour accounting and the binding constraint

Generated by `python -m analysis.labour`. Analytic model of the rules in `kaggriculture.py`,
with pool depths imported from A1. Yields cross-check against the README's published figures
(wheat 4 unfertilized, carrot 3, melon 6, ongoing crops 4).

The watering schedule assumed is the cheapest one that survives: water the planting day
(a fresh seed starts at `consecutive_unwatered = 1`), coast on alternate days until the bonus
window opens at `ceil(max_yield_day / 2)`, then water every day inside it. Watering outside
that window buys survival only.

**The `$/action` column is a trap read on its own.** Sheep look like the best use of a worker
turn in the game at $63.72 — but the entire wool pool is 44 sheep-days deep, which is one and
a half sheep for one season. Melon at $92.56 is the best single number on the board and 26
plants exhaust it. These are *grabs*, not strategies.

Only two rows have infinite capacity: **wheat and geese.** Everything else is a bounded raid.

## What actually binds

| hands | worker-turns/day | hire cost/day | geese supported by labour | geese fitting in 4 quadrants |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 24 | $0 | 4.4 | 44.4 |
| 4 | 120 | $7 | 21.8 | 44.4 |
| 8 | 216 | $54 | 39.3 | 44.4 |
| 12 | 312 | $376 | 56.7 | 44.4 |

(Geese are the yardstick because they're the densest sustainable use of a worker turn. Each is
charged its own daily actions plus the labour to grow the wheat it eats.)

**Labour binds below ~10 hands; land binds above.** The crossover sits at nine or ten hands,
where hiring still costs $143/day — noise against what those workers earn. So the answer to
"what's the binding constraint" is: *land*, provided you hire properly and buy all three
expansions ($7,000 total).

This kills the current baseline agent's design. It hires 8 hands and works 25 tiles, so it is
labour-saturated on a quarter of the board it could own.

## Consequences for strategy

1. **Geese are the engine.** $25.60/action, unbounded, and the wheat they eat is a crop we
   already grow. At the land-bound ceiling of ~44 geese that is roughly $2,270/day once
   running.
2. **Fertilizer is a 16-animal problem.** The pool is 493 units and zero-regenerating, so 16
   animals exhaust it in a season. Past that, `COLLECT_FERTILIZER` is a wasted action and
   animal economics drop to product-only. The current $/action figures include it; they should
   be re-read without it for any fleet above ~16.
3. **Premium crops are opening moves.** Melon's 26 plants and wool's 44 animal-days are worth
   taking early — before the opponent, since the pools are shared — and worth abandoning once
   drained. Whether they're worth *contesting* is A4.
4. **Buy land aggressively.** $7,000 total for 4× the ceiling, against a baseline that
   currently ends games with ~$6,700 unspent capacity.

## Caveat carried forward

`tiles_per_goose` assumes feed wheat is grown, not bought. Buying wheat frees ~1.25 tiles per
goose but drains market inventory, and wheat's *below*-`I0` curve is `sqrt` with target 0.80 —
steep. Whether to grow or buy feed is a genuine optimisation, and it is the first thing A3's
LP should settle.
